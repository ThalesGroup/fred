# Grounded research

1. Identify the question, relevant scope and source constraints from the request
   or conversation. Ask only for missing details that change the research target.
2. Use available search and retrieval tools. Read the underlying passages before
   citing them; search snippets and document titles alone are leads. Prefer the
   original source for a claim and check dates for time-sensitive information.
3. Collect evidence for the central claims. If sources disagree, check whether
   they concern different dates, populations or definitions. Preserve unresolved
   disagreement and identify the scope of each claim.
4. Stop when the central claims have supporting evidence, or when further
   retrieval is unavailable or repeats the same evidence. State material gaps.
5. Answer the question directly. Place the available source links or platform
   citations near the claims they support. Distinguish sourced facts, inferences
   and unknowns. If no sources can be read, explain that research is incomplete
   rather than presenting background knowledge as verified research.
