# Complex or fidelity-sensitive summaries

1. Identify each source's author, date, version and status when available.
   Read the sections needed for the requested scope with available readers;
   track inaccessible or omitted material rather than claiming full coverage.
2. Group conclusions by topic while retaining their source. Check whether an
   apparent disagreement comes from different periods, populations, definitions
   or proposal/approval status. Preserve unresolved disagreements and distinct
   viewpoints instead of creating an artificial consensus.
3. Keep qualifications beside the claims they limit: exclusions, thresholds,
   obligations, exceptions, units, dates and uncertainty. Preserve numerical
   relationships; distinguish quoted source figures from independently checked
   calculations. Use available citations or source/section labels for traceability.
4. Reconcile the final summary with those claims and qualifications. Explain
   material gaps or conflicts within the user's requested format and length;
   a short output can still retain the caveat that changes its conclusion.
