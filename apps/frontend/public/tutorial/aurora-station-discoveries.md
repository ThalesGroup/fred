# Aurora Research Station — Discovery Log

This document is a sample for the Fred tutorial. **Every person, place, date and
result below is invented**: an agent can only answer questions about it by
searching this document, never from what it already knows.

The Aurora Research Station stands on Kelvar Island, in the fictional Brannock
Sea. It was founded on 2 February 2029 by the Halvorsen Foundation and is
directed by Prof. Edda Moreau.

## Biology

### 2031 — The Morrow effect

Dr. Ilse Varnhagen observed that the alga _Lumaria noctis_ glows blue, at a
wavelength of 412 nanometres, whenever the water falls below 3 °C. She named
the phenomenon the Morrow effect, after the station's first diver, Peter Morrow.

### 2038 — A bacterium that digests plastic

A team led by Prof. Malik Esterhazy isolated _Ferrobacter aurorae_, a
bacterium found in the station's harbour mud. In laboratory tanks it breaks
down a PET bottle in 11 days at 30 °C.

## Medicine

### 2033 — The Halden vaccine against grey fever

Grey fever is a respiratory illness that reached Kelvar Island in 2032. Dr.
Tomasz Okafor developed the Halden vaccine, named after the station's
laboratory wing. It was first tested on 9 March 2033 on 14 volunteers from the
island's fishing crews. The trial showed 91 % protection after two doses given
three weeks apart.

### 2037 — The cold-chain capsule

Nurse Amara Diallo designed a capsule that keeps vaccines between 2 °C and 8 °C
for 96 hours without electricity. Its first use was a vaccination round on the
neighbouring island of Sorrel in October 2037.

## Earth and sea

### 2034 — Cerulite, a new mineral

The submersible _Petrel-2_, piloted by Ana Lindqvist, brought back a sky-blue
mineral from the Brannock Trench, at a depth of 2,140 metres. The station
named it cerulite. It is harder than quartz and dissolves in vinegar.

## Astronomy

### 2036 — Comet Okoye-Rask

Juno Rask, a 17-year-old apprentice at the station's observatory, spotted a new
comet on 23 August 2036 while checking images taken by astronomer Daniel
Okoye. It was catalogued as C/2036 R4 and named Okoye-Rask. It returns every
71 years.

## Awards

In 2040, Dr. Ilse Varnhagen received the Hartmann Prize for her work on the
Morrow effect, the first award of its kind for a scientist of the station.
